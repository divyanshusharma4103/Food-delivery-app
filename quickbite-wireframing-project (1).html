1. User persona

Based on assumed research: interviews and a short survey with college students who order food 2–4 times a week.

A

Aarav Verma

22, B.Tech student
Meerut, Uttar Pradesh
Hostel resident, ₹3,000/month food budget

Goals

  • Order a filling meal in under 2 minutes
  • Stay within a small budget
  • Know exactly when food will arrive

Frustrations

  • Hidden delivery and tax charges at checkout
  • Too many steps to re-order a favourite
  • Delivery time estimates that are wrong

Behaviour

  • Orders mostly at night, on a phone
  • Uses UPI for every payment
  • Sorts by price and rating first

Needs from the app

  • Clear total price before paying
  • One-tap re-order
  • Live order tracking

4. Low-fidelity wireframes

Six core screens on a 9:17 phone frame. Grey boxes are content, dashed boxes are images, yellow is the primary action.

5. Design documentation

Problem

Students want a fast, low-cost way to order food, but existing apps hide charges until the last step and bury re-ordering.

Goal

Let a returning user go from opening the app to a placed order in under 2 minutes, with no surprise costs.

Scope

  • Platform: mobile (Android and iOS)
  • Fidelity: low, grayscale wireframes
  • Tool: Figma or hand sketches
  • Screens: 6 core screens

Key design decisions

  • Price filters sit at the top of Home, matching how Aarav searches.
  • A sticky cart bar keeps the next step visible while browsing.
  • Checkout shows the full bill on one screen.
  • Bottom navigation keeps main areas within thumb reach.

Usability principles applied

  • Visibility of system status: live tracking and ETA
  • Recognition over recall: recent orders and one-tap re-order
  • Error prevention: payment retry returns to checkout
  • Consistency: yellow always means the main action

Visual system for later stages

Saffron yellow for primary actions
Charcoal for text
Warm grey for placeholders

Typeface: Schibsted Grotesk (fallback: system sans). Minimum touch target 44px.

Next steps
